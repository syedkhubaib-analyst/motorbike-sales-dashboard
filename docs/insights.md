# Insights & Recommendations:

All figures come from the three PivotTables in the workbook (unfiltered view, 1,000 customers).
**Purchase rate** = customers who bought ÷ all customers in that group. It is the simple division of the Yes count by the Grand Total shown in each PivotTable.

**Overall: 481 of 1,000 customers (48.1 %) bought a bike.**

## 1. Average income by gender and purchase:
| Gender | Did not buy | Bought | Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Female | 53,440 | 55,774 | +2,334 (+4.4 %) |
| Male | 56,208 | 60,124 | +3,916 (+7.0 %) |
| All | 54,875 | 57,963 | +3,088 |

- Buyers have a higher average income than non-buyers in both genders.
- Men have a higher average income than women in both the buyer and non-buyer groups.

## 2. Purchases by age range:
| Age range | Did not buy | Bought | Total | Purchase rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Middle Age (31-54) | 318 | 383 | 701 | **54.6 %** |
| Old (55+) | 130 | 59 | 189 | 31.2 % |
| Young (under 31) | 71 | 39 | 110 | 35.5 % |

- Middle-aged customers are the only age group where more customers bought than did not.
- They account for 383 of 481 buyers (**79.6 %**).

## 3. Purchases by commute distance:
| Commute | Did not buy | Bought | Total | Purchase rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0-1 Miles | 166 | 200 | 366 | 54.6 % |
| 1-2 Miles | 92 | 77 | 169 | 45.6 % |
| 2-5 Miles | 67 | 95 | 162 | **58.6 %** |
| 5-10 Miles | 116 | 76 | 192 | 39.6 % |
| More than 10 Miles | 78 | 33 | 111 | 29.7 % |

- Buyers outnumber non-buyers at 0-1 and 2-5 miles; non-buyers outnumber buyers at 1-2, 5-10 and more than 10 miles.
- The 0-1 mile group has the most buyers (200); the 2-5 mile group has the highest purchase rate (58.6 %).
- Purchase rate is lowest beyond 10 miles (29.7 %).

## Recommendations:
1. **Focus marketing on customers aged 31-54**: they are the largest group and convert best.
2. **Target customers who live within 5 miles** (0-1 and 2-5 miles), where purchase rates are highest. Treat 1-2 miles as a secondary segment.
3. **Test offers for Young and Old customers**, whose purchase rates are about a third. Run a small pilot before scaling.
4. **De-prioritise customers living more than 10 miles away**: they have the lowest purchase rate.
5. **Use the Region, Education and Marital Status slicers** to see whether these patterns hold within each group before choosing campaign audiences.
